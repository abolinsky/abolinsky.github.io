It's the early 2000s, and all your friends are talking about video games. Your parents won't buy you a console, but they've made you a deal: build one yourself, and it's yours to keep.

Walt, the retired ham radio operator down the street, has lent you a pair of walkie-talkies and offered to talk you through it. He'll explain why things work before you build them, but he won't hand you the answers.

Starting from relays, you wire up logic gates, then adders, memory and a CPU of your own, and finally a handheld console. Then you write the games, flash them onto cartridges and link up to play with your friend across the street.

By the end you'll understand how a computer works, from the hardware up through the software, with skills that carry over to the real thing. No experience needed.
